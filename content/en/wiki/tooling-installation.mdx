# Slitter Tooling Installation: Step-by-Step Assembly, Changeover and Common Mistakes

Installing slitter tooling looks like sliding knives and spacers onto an arbor and tightening a nut. In reality it is precision assembly. Every detail — how clean the arbor is, whether spacer thickness is measured, the order of the knives, the clamping force — directly sets strip width accuracy and edge quality.

One careless installation can scrap a whole coil, while a standardized changeover procedure cuts changeover time sharply and makes every setup consistent.

This is a standard operating procedure (SOP) for operators and engineers.

---

## Before the Changeover

### Check the Order and Tooling

Before starting, confirm:

1. **Work order:** strip widths, material and thickness for this run
2. **Knife condition:** the knives are reground and their measurement records are complete
3. **Spacer selection:** spacer combinations for each width are calculated, using the measured thickness of each spacer

### Prepare the Tools

| Tool | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Micrometer (0.001 mm resolution) | Knife OD and spacer thickness |
| Dial indicator with magnetic base | Arbor runout after assembly |
| Lint-free cloths and cleaner | Cleaning the arbor and parts |
| Hydraulic pump (for hydraulic nuts) | Clamping the stack |
| Torque wrench (for conventional nuts) | Tightening to the specified torque |
| 10× loupe | Inspecting knife edges |
| Handling fixtures (if available) | Lifting and positioning knives |

## Removing the Tooling

### Step 1: Stop and Make Safe

1. Make sure the slitter has stopped completely and main power is isolated (lockout/tagout)
2. Wait for the arbors to stop on their own — never brake them by hand or with tools

### Step 2: Release the Clamping

**Hydraulic nut:**
1. Connect the pump to the nut's filling port
2. Open the release valve slowly — not fully at once
3. When the pressure reaches zero, unscrew the nut by hand

**Conventional nut:**
1. Loosen the nut (counterclockwise) with a high-torque tool
2. Support the weight of the stack so it cannot drop suddenly

### Step 3: Record the Current Setup

Before removing anything, photograph the current stack, including:
- The order and orientation of spacers — especially important with asymmetrical parts
- Stripper ring positions
- Positions of any special parts

### Step 4: Remove Parts in Order

Remove parts in the reverse order of installation:
1. Nut → 2. End spacer → 3. Knives and spacers, from the free end toward the datum end → 4. Stripper rings

**Lay parts out on the bench in the order removed — do not mix them.** If the same setup will be reused, reassemble in reverse order.

## Installing the Tooling

### Step 1: Clean the Arbor

**This is the most important step, and the one most often skipped.**

With a lint-free cloth and cleaner, clean:
- The seating surface where knives and spacers sit
- The thread — remove chips and old thread locker
- The datum face that locates the stack

Wipe dry and check that nothing remains.

**Why:** a 0.01 mm particle or chip left on the arbor is clamped under a spacer and acts like extra local thickness, shifting the stack position.

### Step 2: Measure Every Part

Measure and record each part before installation:

| Part | Measure | Why |
|------|---------|-----|
| Each knife | OD and thickness | Pair OD difference ≤ 0.002 mm |
| Each spacer | Thickness, three-point average | Calculate cumulative thickness and confirm widths |
| Stripper rings | OD, to check extension | Extension within the working range |

Add up the cumulative spacer thickness and compare it with the target widths on the work order. Start installing only when it matches.

### Step 3: Install the Stack (Upper and Lower Arbors Separately)

**Order, from the datum end:**

```
Datum ring → first set (large spacer + knife + large spacer) → second set → ... → end spacer → nut or hydraulic nut
```

**Techniques:**

**1. Fewest, largest spacers:** build each position from as few spacers as possible, thickest first, to reduce sources of accumulated error.

**2. Knife orientation:** check the cutting direction. Some knives, especially those with angled side faces, must face a particular way, and upper and lower knives must match.

**3. Even seating:** seat each part squarely on the arbor. A tilted knife or spacer creates local contact stress and spoils accuracy.

**4. Stripper ring position:** fit stripper rings on the outer side of the knife, away from the strip contact face, and check the extension.

### Step 4: Clamp the Stack

**Hydraulic nut:**
1. Thread the nut on fully by hand
2. Check that the nut face contacts the stack face evenly
3. Connect the pump and pressurize slowly to the set pressure in the machine manual
4. Close the shut-off valve and disconnect the pump

**Conventional nut:**
1. Thread the nut on fully by hand
2. Tighten with a torque wrench to the specified torque from the machine manual
3. Tighten in two stages: first to 80% of the torque, then to full torque

### Step 5: Verify After Assembly

**Do not skip this step.**

**5-1: Measure knife runout (TIR)**

Measure the radial runout of each knife with a dial indicator:

Recommended knife runout after assembly: TIR within 0.005 mm (same for tool steel and carbide knives).

If runout is over the limit: remove the knives, clean the arbor and knives, check for debris and reassemble.

**5-2: Check the clearance**

Check the horizontal clearance of each knife pair with a dial indicator against the set value — do not rely on the calculation alone.

**5-3: Check stripper rings visually**

Make sure every stripper ring has an even extension and is not tilted.

## Threading and Test Cut

### Threading

1. Thread the strip through the stack at low speed (5–10 m/min)
2. Check that every strip runs on its own path, with no crossing
3. Check that the edge trim runs correctly
4. Increase slowly to 30–50 m/min and slit 10–20 m

### Test Cut Checks

Take samples from the test length and measure:
- The actual width of every strip, against the work order tolerance
- Burr height at each cut
- Any abnormality on the strip edges

Accelerate to production speed only when everything is within specification.

## Shortening Changeover Time

### 1. Pre-Assemble a Spare Stack

While the current order runs, assemble the next order's stack on a spare arbor, with spacers measured and calculated. At changeover, swap arbors instead of building the stack from scratch. This is usually the single largest time saving.

### 2. Build Spacer Kits

For widths you run often, keep a dedicated spacer kit for each, marked with color codes or tags. At changeover, take the matching kit instead of recalculating and picking spacers every time.

### 3. Standardize the Measurement Sheet

Use a standard changeover sheet with:
- Knife OD checks
- Cumulative spacer thickness calculation
- Runout after assembly
- Test cut widths

Operators fill it in every time, which keeps changeovers consistent and leaves a traceable record.

### 4. Use Lightweight Spacers

Handling a heavy stack is a large part of changeover time. Lightweight spacers can reduce the weight of a 20-spacer stack substantially, cutting handling time and operator effort. See [Lightweight vs Standard Arbor Spacers](/en/wiki/lightweight-spacers).

## Common Installation Mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence | Prevention |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| Arbor not cleaned thoroughly | Spacers mis-positioned, widths out of tolerance | Clean at every changeover |
| Using nominal instead of measured thickness | Accumulated error out of tolerance | Measure every spacer |
| Knives not checked as pairs | Large OD difference within a pair, uneven cut | Measure pair OD; difference ≤ 0.002 mm |
| Skipping the runout check | Periodic burr, poor quality | Always measure TIR after assembly |
| Setting clearance by calculation only | Actual clearance differs from setting | Confirm with a dial indicator |
| Insufficient nut clamping | Knives shift, widths drift | Use the specified torque or hydraulic pressure |
| Stripper ring fitted the wrong way | Poor stripping, strip wraps | Check orientation before fitting |

## Safety

- **Knife edges are extremely sharp** — wear cut-resistant gloves during installation
- Never let a knife edge touch anything hard, including the floor or the bench, or it will chip
- Never exceed the maximum pressure when using hydraulic equipment
- Lift large stacks (OD over 200 mm) with a hoist or two people — never alone

## FAQ

**Q: Edge quality is worse after a knife change — what is the likely cause?**
(1) Arbor not cleaned; (2) new knife OD changed the clearance and it was not re-checked; (3) runout out of tolerance and not measured. Check in that order.

**Q: How do I confirm spacer thickness?**
Micrometer (0.001 mm resolution), three points, averaged. Always use the measured value, never the nominal.

**Q: How do I get changeover under 30 minutes?**
Hydraulic nuts, a pre-assembled spare stack and lightweight spacers together.

**Q: Runout is out of tolerance after assembly — what now?**
Remove the knives, clean the arbor seating faces, reassemble and measure again. If still out, check the knife bores for wear.
